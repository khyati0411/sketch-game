## Context

US-010 offers the drawer three words from "a built-in English word list for families", with no repeats within a game. US-011 shows the word as blanks, and US-012 recognizes correct guesses (for example "Cat " matches "cat"). The owner chose a list written for this project, about 300 words.

## Options considered

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Claude writes a list for the project (chosen) | No licensing questions; tuned to be drawable and family-friendly; reviewed in the PR | Takes curation effort |
| An existing public list | Larger | Licensing to check; many words are hard to draw or unsuitable for children |
| About 100 words now | Quick | Repeats across games sooner; three rounds × 8 players × 3 choices already uses 72 words |

## Decision

**The list** is `shared/words/en.json`, an array of lowercase strings with these rules (checked by a unit test):
- 3 to 14 characters, letters a–z, and at most one space (so "ice cream" is allowed);
- no duplicates;
- concrete things a child could draw: animals, food, objects, places, nature, transport, people and jobs, sports;
- nothing scary, rude or brand-named.

**Choosing.** The server picks three different words that haven't been offered earlier in the game. If the unused pool runs out, it resets.

**The mask.** Each letter shows as `_` and a space shows as a gap, so "ice cream" is `_ _ _   _ _ _ _ _`. Clients also show the letter count.

**Matching.** `normalizeGuess(text)`:
- trims;
- lowercases;
- strips diacritics (NFD, then removes marks);
- collapses runs of whitespace to one space.

A guess is correct when its normalized form equals the normalized word. There's no "close guess" hint in R1; that's R2.

## Consequences

- With about 300 words, a full 8-player game uses at most 72 offered words, so words don't repeat within a game.
- Custom word lists in R2 reuse the same rules and `normalizeGuess`.
- The owner reviews the list in this PR, and any word can be removed later without code changes.
